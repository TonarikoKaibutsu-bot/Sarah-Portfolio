Case Study · LIMS / Adoption

Improving post-go-live ownership for a laboratory-facing system

Clarifying support paths so routine questions, technical issues, and adoption gaps reach the right people.

The challenge

A successful launch still needs a support structure.

A laboratory-facing system was implemented to improve visibility, audit trails, and task monitoring across multiple stakeholder groups.

After go-live, adoption was inconsistent. Some teams used the intended support structure, while another larger user group continued routing routine questions directly to laboratory subject matter experts and, at times, employees who had not been trained to provide system support.

The impact

Support gaps created hidden operational costs.

  • Subject matter experts were pulled into routine usage support instead of higher-value improvement work.
  • Users were unclear on where different types of questions should go.
  • Leadership adoption and ownership were inconsistent.
  • Recurring questions were not always visible to the broader project team as potential training or adoption signals.

My role

Separate the symptom from the underlying support problem.

I identified the breakdown in the post-go-live support path and worked to distinguish routine usage questions from true software defects, system limitations, and broader training or ownership gaps.

I encouraged subject matter experts to surface recurring questions rather than absorbing them individually, helping make adoption issues more visible to the people responsible for supporting the implementation.

I also communicated the operational impact of the support breakdown to leadership and other appropriate stakeholders.

The support model

Route each problem to the level best equipped to solve it.

  1. 01 Observe recurring behavior

    Identify repeated questions, workarounds, or support requests after launch.

  2. 02 Classify the issue

    Determine whether the problem is software, training, communication, process design, or ownership.

  3. 03 Clarify first-line support

    Define where routine user questions should be handled before escalating.

  4. 04 Escalate true system issues

    Route confirmed defects or system limitations to the project team, vendor, or appropriate technical owner.

  5. 05 Use recurring issues as feedback

    Treat repeated questions and workarounds as continuous-improvement signals rather than isolated interruptions.

Desired future state

Make ownership visible after the project goes live.

The goal is a clearer support model where routine questions are handled through appropriate local support channels, recurring adoption gaps are visible to the project team, and subject matter experts are reserved for true technical issues, workflow decisions, and process improvement.

Post-go-live support is not separate from implementation. It is part of whether the implementation succeeds.
