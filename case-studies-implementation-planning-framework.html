Building an implementation planning framework for clearer projects

Creating a shared structure for moving projects from idea to implementation.

The challenge

Good projects can still start with unclear foundations.

Projects can begin moving forward before teams have clearly defined the goal, stakeholder ownership, requirements, minimum viable product, or success criteria.

Without a shared planning framework, feature requests can be mistaken for requirements, adoption planning can happen too late, and verification or validation steps can be missed.

My role

Build a practical structure leaders could reuse.

I created a practical implementation planning framework designed to help leaders and project stakeholders ask the right questions before work begins.

Rather than prescribing a specific project-management methodology, the framework provides a repeatable structure for defining what is being built, why it matters, who owns it, and how success will be evaluated.

The framework

Ten questions from discovery through continuous improvement.

  1. 01 Discovery

    What is the overall goal?

  2. 02 Stakeholder buy-in

    Who is requesting, supporting, approving, and owning the work?

  3. 03 Requirements

    What must the project accomplish?

  4. 04 Minimum viable product

    What minimum functionality must exist for users to succeed?

  5. 05 Feature requests

    What would be useful, but is not required for success?

  6. 06 Success criteria

    How will we know the implementation succeeded?

  7. 07 User acceptance testing

    Where and how will users evaluate the solution?

  8. 08 Deployment

    How will training, communication, and rollout occur?

  9. 09 Validation & verification

    Did we build the right thing, and does it work as intended?

  10. 10 Continuous improvement

    What feedback loop exists after launch?

Why it matters

Make assumptions visible before they become rework.

The framework creates a shared vocabulary for implementation work and helps separate requirements from enhancements, deployment from adoption, and completion from demonstrated success.

It is designed to make project assumptions visible early, when they are easier to discuss, test, and change.
