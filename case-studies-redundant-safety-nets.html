Case Study · Process Improvement

Reducing duplicate work by reviewing legacy safety nets

Evaluating whether existing controls were still protecting the process or simply adding redundant workload after system improvements.

The challenge

Controls should protect the process, not survive by default.

A team continued using legacy checks after a newer system introduced improved monitoring, reporting, and traceability.

Some of those controls had originally served an important purpose, but changes to the system raised a new question: were they still controlling meaningful risk, or had they become duplicate work?

My role

Review the work without weakening the controls that matter.

I partnered with a counterpart at another facility to review the current workflow and identify which safety nets still protected quality and which could potentially be removed or redesigned.

The goal was not simply to eliminate steps. It was to reduce unnecessary work while maintaining food-safety protections, traceability, and audit readiness.

Evaluation framework

Start with the risk, then evaluate the control.

  1. 01 Identify the original risk

    What risk was this safety net originally designed to control?

  2. 02 Compare against current controls

    Does the current system already control, detect, or monitor that risk?

  3. 03 Define the evidence

    What evidence would justify removing, changing, or retaining the step?

  4. 04 Confirm ownership and approval

    Who needs to review and approve the proposed workflow change?

  5. 05 Verify effectiveness

    How will we confirm the revised workflow continues to control the risk?

Desired future state

Keep the controls that add value. Remove the ones that no longer do.

The target state is a leaner workflow where employees perform controls that meaningfully protect quality and food safety, while redundant work is removed through a documented, reviewed, and approved change process.

Process improvement is not about removing safeguards. It is about making sure each safeguard still has a purpose.
